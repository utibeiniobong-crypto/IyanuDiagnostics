Theological background & balance notes

The Bible Study Pack's "balance box" notes — on territorial spirits, "positive confession"/Word of Faith theology, and generational curses — reflect widely documented positions within mainstream evangelical and charismatic theological discussion rather than a single cited source. Readers wanting to go deeper on any of these debates are encouraged to consult a range of authors across the traditions represented above, rather than relying on any single ministry's framing, including this app's.

Sources deliberately not used

Sources considered but not incorporated into the underlying research for this app:

  • TB Joshua / Synagogue, Church of All Nations (SCOAN) — excluded following a January 2024 BBC Africa Eye investigation documenting serious abuse allegations from more than 25 former members.
  • A set of six user-supplied documents describing additional "spiritual laws" and case studies attributed to named real authors — not used as source material, since their citation lists resolved to generic homepages (e.g. facebook.com, youtube.com) rather than to any identifiable article or book passage, a pattern consistent with fabricated attribution.
  • "Spirituals," a narrative drama film series by The Winlos — reviewed via a user-supplied summary document, but not incorporated as source material. "Spirituals" is fiction, not testimony.
  • Daniel Adams (The Supernatural Life) — excluded following multiple independent investigations (Protestia, The Apostasy Files) documenting staged deliverance demonstrations using recurring actors across supposedly unrelated sessions, a YouTube sanction for purchased views, a $500/hour paid deliverance-consultation model, and close working ties to TB Joshua and other already-excluded or contested figures in this space.
  • "Overcoming Spiritual Attacks from Home" (attributed to "Pastor Rich Living-Proof Anyiam") — a user-supplied summary described this as a real, catalogued 2010 book, but no trace of it exists in any library catalog, bookseller, or publisher record. The summary document itself contained an impossible anachronism (describing a 2010 book as mapping to this app's 2026 checkboxes), consistent with fabricated source material.
