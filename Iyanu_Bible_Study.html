Session 0
Foundation
Identity and Authority in Christ
"Behold, I give unto you power to tread on serpents and scorpions, and over all the power of the enemy: and nothing shall by any means hurt you." — Luke 10:19, KJV
Why start here

Every session that follows deals with a specific way the enemy is believed to operate. But none of that teaching is safe to build on without first being clear about one thing: who you are in Christ, and what authority has already been given to you. This session exists to put the foundation in the right place before anything else is built on it.

Key texts
2 Corinthians 5:17, KJVTherefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.
Colossians 1:13-14, KJVWho hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of his dear Son: In whom we have redemption through his blood, even the forgiveness of sins.
2 Corinthians 10:3-5, KJVFor though we walk in the flesh, we do not war after the flesh: (For the weapons of our warfare are not carnal, but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds;) Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ.
1 John 4:4, KJVYe are of God, little children, and have overcome them: because greater is he that is in you, than he that is in the world.
Teaching

Two things are true at the same time in the New Testament's picture of the Christian life: there is a real spiritual conflict, and the believer is not the underdog in it. Colossians describes a transfer that has already happened, out of the power of darkness and into the kingdom of the Son, as a completed fact, not a future hope. Authority is not something a believer works up to; it is something given, the way Luke 10:19 describes Jesus giving it to His followers before sending them out.

This matters for how the rest of this study should be read. None of the teaching ahead is meant to be read as though the believer is fundamentally vulnerable and only barely holding the line. It's the opposite: these sessions describe obstacles to a freedom that is already legally secured in Christ, the way a title deed can be secure even while someone still has to physically move a squatter off the property.

Discussion questions
  1. Before this study, did you tend to think about spiritual warfare more from a place of fear or from a place of confidence? What shaped that?
  2. Colossians 1:13 describes deliverance from darkness as something that has "already" happened for the believer. How does that change the way you'd pray about an ongoing struggle?
  3. What is the difference between healthy watchfulness (1 Peter 5:8) and living in a state of fear about the enemy?
  4. Who is someone in your life who models spiritual authority without anxiety? What do you notice about how they talk about spiritual struggle?
Reflection

Write out, privately, anything this session brought to mind that you want to bring to God in prayer.

Closing declaration I am a new creation in Christ. I have been delivered from the power of darkness and brought into the kingdom of the Son. The authority I need for this study has already been given to me — I do not need to fight for a position I already hold.

Session 1
The Law of Legal Rights
What Gives the Enemy Ground
"Neither give place to the devil." — Ephesians 4:27, KJV
Key texts
James 4:7, KJVSubmit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you.
1 Peter 5:8-9, KJVBe sober, be vigilant; because your adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour: Whom resist stedfast in the faith.
Psalm 66:18, KJVIf I regard iniquity in my heart, the LORD will not hear me.
1 John 1:9, KJVIf we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
Matthew 5:23-24, KJVTherefore if thou bring thy gift to the altar, and there rememberest that thy brother hath ought against thee; Leave there thy gift before the altar, and go thy way; first be reconciled to thy brother, and then come and offer thy gift.
Teaching

The Law of Legal Rights, as taught in this tradition, holds that unconfessed sin, unforgiveness, and unresolved wrongdoing function like an open door, not because God is looking for a reason to punish, but because these things genuinely damage the relationships and posture that Scripture ties to spiritual protection and clarity. Psalm 66:18 makes a direct connection between regarded (harbored) sin and a sense of God's silence.

Matthew 5:23-24 is worth sitting with here: Jesus interrupts an act of worship to prioritize reconciliation with another person. Whatever else this teaches, it establishes that unresolved relational wrongs are not a private, spiritually neutral matter.

🎧 Teaching audio/video for this session — not yet added.
Discussion questions
  1. Is there a person you are currently holding something against? What would it look like, practically, to begin the process Matthew 5:23-24 describes?
  2. Psalm 66:18 links "regarding iniquity" with unanswered prayer. What's the difference between briefly struggling with sin and "regarding" or nursing it?
  3. What is something you have been reluctant to confess, even to God, and what does 1 John 1:9 promise about that moment?
  4. Is there unlawfully or dishonestly gained property, money, or advantage in your life that this session brings to mind?
A point worth knowing Not all Christian traditions frame ongoing struggle primarily in terms of "legal ground" given to the enemy. Many pastoral counselors would say the same passages point simply to the ordinary, well-established connection between unconfessed sin, unforgiveness, and a troubled conscience or damaged relationships, without needing a courtroom metaphor to explain why those things matter.
Reflection

Use this space to write out anything this session brought to mind that you want to bring to God in prayer.

Closing declaration Lord, I choose to walk in the light, holding nothing back from You. I forgive as I have been forgiven, and I release anything I have been holding onto that does not belong in Your hands.

Session 2
The Law of Altars & Territorial Spirits
What We Build Our Lives Around
"Throw down the altar of Baal that thy father hath, and cut down the grove that is by it." — Judges 6:25, KJV
Key texts
Deuteronomy 7:5, KJVBut thus shall ye deal with them; ye shall destroy their altars, and break down their images, and cut down their groves, and burn their graven images with fire.
Joshua 24:15, KJVAnd if it seem evil unto you to serve the LORD, choose you this day whom ye will serve... but as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD.
Genesis 35:2-3, KJVThen Jacob said unto his household... Put away the strange gods that are among you, and be clean, and change your garments: And let us arise, and go up to Bethel; and I will make there an altar unto God.
1 Corinthians 10:20-21, KJVBut I say, that the things which the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to devils, and not to God: and I would not that ye should have fellowship with devils. Ye cannot drink the cup of the Lord, and the cup of devils.
Teaching

An altar, in the plain sense of the biblical text, is simply the place where sacrifice and devotion happen, where a person or household says, in effect, "this is what we build our life around." This tradition applies that pattern to physical shrines, charms, and objects connected to occult or idolatrous practice.

Jacob's household in Genesis 35 offers a useful pattern for what to do about this: identify what doesn't belong, physically remove it, and replace the old altar with a new act of devotion to God.

It's worth being honest that "territorial spirits" is a more specific and more debated claim than the general principle of not giving devotion to idols.

🎧 Teaching audio/video for this session — not yet added.
Discussion questions
  1. Setting aside literal shrines, what does your life currently seem to be "built around"? Where does your time, money, and attention actually go?
  2. Is there an object, place, or practice in your family history that this session brings to mind?
  3. Jacob didn't just remove the old gods, he built a new altar. What would "building a new altar" look like practically in your own life or home?
  4. How do you personally hold the tension between taking spiritual influence seriously and not becoming fearful of places or objects?
A point worth knowing The concept of geographically-assigned "territorial spirits" that can be identified and addressed by name became prominent through the "strategic-level spiritual warfare" teaching associated with missiologist C. Peter Wagner. It is influential but also one of the more contested ideas within charismatic and Pentecostal circles.
Reflection
Closing declaration Lord, reveal anything I have unknowingly built my life around that does not belong there. I choose You as the center of my household, and I dedicate what remains to You alone.

Session 3
The Law of Marine / Water Spirits
Chaos, the Sea, and the God Who Speaks Peace
"In that day the LORD... shall punish leviathan the piercing serpent... and he shall slay the dragon that is in the sea." — Isaiah 27:1, KJV
Key texts
Genesis 1:2, KJVAnd the earth was without form, and void; and darkness was upon the face of the deep. And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.
Psalm 107:28-29, KJVThen they cry unto the LORD in their trouble, and he bringeth them out of their distresses. He maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.
Mark 4:39, KJVAnd he arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm.
Revelation 21:1, KJVAnd I saw a new heaven and a new earth: for the first heaven and the first earth were passed away; and there was no more sea.
Teaching

Throughout Scripture, the sea and "the deep" are used as recurring images of chaos and what is beyond human control, from the formless deep in Genesis 1, to the storm the disciples faced in Mark 4, to the symbolic absence of any sea at all in Revelation 21. This tradition's teaching on "marine spirits" takes that symbolic thread and treats it more literally, as spirits operating from actual bodies of water.

Whatever view a reader takes on the more specific claims, the consistent pattern across every one of these passages is the same: it is God, not the chaos itself, who has the final word.

🎧 Teaching audio/video for this session — not yet added.
Discussion questions
  1. Where in your life right now does something feel like "the sea," chaotic, beyond your control, or frightening?
  2. Mark 4 shows the disciples afraid in the middle of the very storm Jesus was about to calm. What does their fear, even with Jesus present, say to you?
  3. Revelation 21:1 pictures a future with "no more sea." How does having that as a future hope change how you carry a present struggle?
  4. If you have had recurring dreams involving water, what feeling do they tend to leave you with, and have you brought that feeling to God directly?
Reflection
Closing declaration Lord, You spoke peace over the sea, and Your word still holds authority over whatever feels chaotic or out of control in my life.

Session 4
The Law of Agreement
What You Repeat, You Reinforce
"Again I say unto you, That if two of you shall agree on earth as touching any thing that they shall ask, it shall be done for them of my Father which is in heaven." — Matthew 18:19, KJV
Key texts
Genesis 11:4, 6, KJVAnd they said, Go to, let us build us a city and a tower, whose top may reach unto heaven... And the LORD said, Behold, the people is one, and they have all one language; and this they begin to do: and now nothing will be restrained from them, which they have imagined to do.
Numbers 14:1-2, KJVAnd all the congregation lifted up their voice, and cried; and the people wept that night. And all the children of Israel murmured against Moses and against Aaron.
Acts 4:31, KJVAnd when they had prayed, the place was shaken where they were assembled together; and they were all filled with the Holy Ghost, and they spake the word of God with boldness.
Job 2:9-10, KJVThen said his wife unto him, Dost thou still retain thine integrity? curse God, and die. But he said unto her, Thou speakest as one of the foolish women speaketh... In all this did not Job sin with his lips.
Teaching

This law holds that agreement between two or more parties carries real spiritual weight, drawn from Matthew 18:19. In this tradition, that principle is applied in reverse too: when a person responds to fear or a bad report by dwelling on it and expecting it to happen, that response is treated as a kind of agreement that opens a door. The remedy taught here is not suppressing fear through willpower, but consciously choosing agreement with what God has said instead.

Job 2:9-10 is a striking contrast to Numbers 14: in real pain, Job refuses to let his words agree with despair. That refusal, not the absence of suffering, is what Scripture highlights as faithful.

🎧 Teaching audio/video for this session — not yet added.
Discussion questions
  1. Numbers 14 shows an entire community talking itself into despair together. Have you seen a group (family, friends, coworkers) do that? What did it look like?
  2. What is one fear or bad report you have caught yourself repeating out loud recently?
  3. Job refused to agree with his wife's suggestion even in real grief. What's the difference between honestly naming pain and "agreeing" with hopelessness?
  4. Who could you deliberately pray in agreement with about something specific this week?
A point worth knowing Matthew 18:19 sits inside a passage about church discipline and two or three gathering in Jesus' name (Matthew 18:15-20), and many biblical scholars read it as specifically about corporate prayer and church authority, not a general principle that private fear "activates" an attack. It's also worth being careful here: fear and anxiety are often symptoms of trauma or a mental health condition, not a moral failure, and treating a frightened person's fear as the cause of their suffering can add guilt on top of pain that needs compassion, not correction.
Reflection
Closing declaration Lord, I choose to agree with what You have said about me, not with fear, panic, or bad news. Surround me with people who will agree with me in prayer.

Session 5
The Law of the Tongue & Seeds
Words That Outlast the Moment
"Death and life are in the power of the tongue: and they that love it shall eat the fruit thereof." — Proverbs 18:21, KJV
Key texts
Genesis 1:3, KJVAnd God said, Let there be light: and there was light.
Numbers 14:28, KJVSay unto them, As truly as I live, saith the LORD, as ye have spoken in mine ears, so will I do to you.
James 3:9-10, KJVTherewith bless we God, even the Father; and therewith curse we men, which are made after the similitude of God. Out of the same mouth proceedeth blessing and cursing. My brethren, these things ought not so to be.
Genesis 27:33, KJVAnd Isaac trembled very exceedingly, and said, Who? where is he that hath taken venison, and brought it me, and I have eaten of all before thou camest, and have blessed him? yea, and he shall be blessed.
Teaching

Drawing on Proverbs 18:21 and Galatians 6:7, this law treats spoken words as functioning like seeds, carrying real consequence over time. Envious or malicious words spoken by others, and self-defeating words spoken by a person over their own life, are both taken seriously here rather than dismissed as "just talk."

James 3 is a needed check on this teaching taken too far: it doesn't picture the tongue as a neutral tool of spiritual technology, but as something that needs discipline and repentance, "these things ought not so to be."

🎧 Teaching audio/video for this session — not yet added.
Discussion questions
  1. What is a phrase you say about yourself often, without thinking about it? Is it true, and is it helpful?
  2. Who has spoken a word over your life, positive or negative, that you still carry?
  3. James 3 says blessing and cursing shouldn't come from the same mouth. Where do you notice that inconsistency in your own speech?
  4. What would it look like to speak one deliberate, true blessing over someone this week?
A point worth knowing The idea that spoken words carry independent spiritual power to create or destroy outcomes is closely associated with the "Word of Faith" or "positive confession" movement, which many mainstream evangelical theologians have criticized as extending wisdom-literature sayings like Proverbs 18:21 further than the text itself claims. Both perspectives agree words matter and deserve discipline; they differ on whether words function as an independent spiritual force.
Reflection
Closing declaration Lord, set a watch over my mouth. I choose today to speak life over my own future and over the people around me.

Session 6
The Law of Household Authority
Who Has Access, and What They Do With It
"But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD." — Joshua 24:15, KJV
Key texts
1 Samuel 3:13, KJVFor I have told him that I will judge his house for ever for the iniquity which he knoweth; because his sons made themselves vile, and he restrained them not.
Judges 17:5, KJVAnd the man Micah had an house of gods, and made an ephod, and teraphim, and consecrated one of his sons, who became his priest.
Acts 16:31, KJVAnd they said, Believe on the Lord Jesus Christ, and thou shalt be saved, and thy house.
Galatians 6:1, KJVBrethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness; considering thyself, lest thou also be tempted.
Teaching

Based on Joshua 24:15 and the broader biblical pattern of household heads carrying responsibility for those under their care, this law holds that close relational access carries real influence over a household's atmosphere. In deliverance teaching this is sometimes called "household wickedness": a family member using proximity and trust to undermine someone from the inside.

This teaching needs unusual care. Historically, teaching that a household's suffering flows from the spiritual state of its head has sometimes been used to pressure vulnerable family members, especially women and children, into staying in harmful situations, or to imply a victim's suffering is their own responsibility to fix through more submission or more prayer. That is a misuse of the teaching, not a faithful application of it. If household relationships involve actual abuse, coercion, or danger, the appropriate response is safety and outside help, from a pastor, counselor, or where relevant, legal authorities, not more spiritual effort within the household alone.

🎧 Teaching audio/video for this session — not yet added.
Discussion questions
  1. What healthy or unhealthy patterns of authority did you grow up watching in your own household?
  2. Acts 16:31 pictures a household saved together through one person's faith. Who in your life could you be that kind of influence for?
  3. Where is the line, in your own understanding, between legitimate spiritual concern for a family member and control?
  4. If this session brought up something unsafe rather than just difficult, who is a trustworthy outside person you could talk to?
Reflection
Closing declaration Lord, establish healthy, loving order in my household, built on service rather than control. Protect anyone in my care who is vulnerable, and give me the humility to seek help where I need it.

Session 7
The Law of Spiritual Blindness
Staying Awake
"In whom the god of this world hath blinded the minds of them which believe not." — 2 Corinthians 4:4, KJV
Key texts
2 Kings 6:16-17, KJVAnd he answered, Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them. And Elisha prayed, and said, LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see.
Revelation 3:17-18, KJVBecause thou sayest, I am rich, and increased with goods, and have need of nothing; and knowest not that thou art wretched, and miserable, and poor, and blind, and naked... anoint thine eyes with eyesalve, that thou mayest see.
Luke 24:15-16, 31, KJVAnd it came to pass, that, while they communed together and reasoned, Jesus himself drew near, and went with them. But their eyes were holden that they should not know him... And their eyes were opened, and they knew him.
Genesis 3:1, KJVNow the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field which the LORD God had made.
Teaching

This law holds that spiritual vigilance is not optional. A person who stops actively paying attention to their spiritual life becomes more vulnerable to deception or drifting into harmful patterns unnoticed. Revelation 3 is a sobering example: the Laodicean church was blind precisely because it felt secure, "knowest not that thou art wretched."

There's a difference between the healthy watchfulness Scripture calls for and a fearful hypervigilance that sees deception behind every disappointment. The goal, in a healthy reading, is discernment and peace, not suspicion.

🎧 Teaching audio/video for this session — not yet added.
Discussion questions
  1. Elisha's servant needed his eyes opened to see what was already there. Where might you need a similar kind of clarity right now?
  2. The Laodicean church didn't know it was blind. How do you personally check your own blind spots?
  3. Who plays the role of an "outside pair of eyes" in your life, someone who will tell you the truth even when it's uncomfortable?
  4. What's one ordinary practice (Scripture, prayer, honest friendship) you've let slip that this session brings to mind?
Reflection
Closing declaration Lord, wherever I have grown spiritually drowsy, wake me up. Give me discernment, not fear, and surround me with people who will tell me the truth.

Session 8
Living It Out
Staying Free, Not Just Getting Free
"Stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free, and be not entangled again with the yoke of bondage." — Galatians 5:1, KJV
Key texts
Romans 12:2, KJVAnd be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind, that ye may prove what is that good, and acceptable, and perfect, will of God.
Ephesians 6:10-13, KJVFinally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might. Put on the whole armour of God... For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.
Matthew 12:43-45, KJVWhen the unclean spirit is gone out of a man, he walketh through dry places, seeking rest, and findeth none. Then he saith, I will return into my house from whence I came out; and when he is come, he findeth it empty, swept, and garnished. Then goeth he, and taketh with himself seven other spirits more wicked than himself, and they enter in and dwell there.
Galatians 6:1-2, KJVBrethren, if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one in the spirit of meekness... Bear ye one another's burdens, and so fulfil the law of Christ.
Teaching

Matthew 12:43-45 is a sobering passage to end a study like this on, and it's included deliberately: Jesus describes a house that is "swept and garnished," cleaned out, but left empty, and the result is worse than before. Romans 12:2 supplies what the empty house lacks: ongoing, active renewal, not a one-time event.

Ephesians 6's armor is worth noticing for what it's made of: truth, righteousness, the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the word of God, and prayer. It is a description of an ordinary, sustained Christian life. And Galatians 6 insists this was never meant to be walked alone.

Practically, this means the seven-day prayer plans in the app's diagnostic reports are a beginning, not a finish line. What sustains freedom afterward tends to be ordinary: consistent time in Scripture, honest community, and, where the underlying struggle has a medical or psychological dimension, professional care alongside the spiritual.

Discussion questions
  1. What has historically made a season of spiritual breakthrough "stick" for you, or fail to stick?
  2. Which piece of the armor in Ephesians 6 do you currently neglect most?
  3. Who could you ask to help "bear the burden" of whatever this study has surfaced for you?
  4. What's one concrete, ordinary practice you will commit to in the next 30 days?
Reflection
Closing declaration Lord, I don't just want to be free, I want to stay free. Renew my mind daily. Fill what has been emptied. Surround me with people who will walk with me.

Closing Word

This study has followed one specific tradition's framework closely, because that is the framework the Iyanu Diagnostics tool itself is built on. It's worth saying plainly, as this study closes, that this is one lens within a much larger and more varied Christian conversation, not the only faithful way to read these questions, and not one that should be held more tightly than the Scripture it's drawn from.

Two things are worth carrying forward. First: hold the more specific and more debated claims in this framework with some humility. Second: nothing in this study is a substitute for medical or psychological care where that is what's actually needed.

If working through this study brings up thoughts of suicide or self-harm, please reach out right now to a crisis line in your area (in the US: call or text 988) or go to your nearest emergency room. If it surfaces mental health or medical symptoms, please also see a qualified professional alongside anything here.
