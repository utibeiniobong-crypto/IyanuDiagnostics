Symptoms Checker

Before the full diagnostic, a shorter question: do you actually know what you're looking for? Most people don't, at first — and that's the point of this page.

1. How to know your symptoms

In this framework, a "symptom" isn't necessarily a medical one. It's a pattern — something that keeps happening, keeps blocking you, or keeps costing you, without an ordinary explanation that fully accounts for it.

1It repeats despite your effort
A single bad year is life. The same specific kind of setback showing up again and again, no matter how hard you work or how carefully you plan, is a pattern worth naming.
2It doesn't fully add up
Ordinary explanations exist for most hardship — the economy, timing, other people's choices. A true symptom is the piece that's left over after you've honestly accounted for all of that, and it still doesn't add up.
3It has a shape in your family, not just your life
If a specific kind of struggle shows up in your parents, grandparents, or siblings too, not identically but recognizably, that's worth paying attention to as its own category, separate from what's just happening to you personally.
4It shows up in your sleep
A recurring dream, especially one that leaves a specific unsettled feeling behind it, is treated in this tradition as data, not noise. The full checklist and the Other Relevant Dreams page both take this seriously.
2. You might not know you need this

This app exists partly because its own creator didn't recognize his own need for deliverance for years, even while actively growing in faith. It's a common blind spot, not a personal failing: it is much easier to see a pattern in someone else's life than to name it honestly in your own. If nothing on this page feels urgent yet, that isn't proof there's nothing there — it may just mean you haven't looked at your own life the way you'd look at someone else's.

A question worth sitting with If a close friend described your life to you exactly as it is, struggle for struggle, would you recognize a pattern in it that they couldn't see themselves? Now ask the same question about your own life.
3. Look at what persists, not just what happened

Rather than trying to remember every hard thing that's ever happened, work through these areas of life and ask what has followed you, specifically, for years.

Work & finances
What keeps stalling right before it should succeed?
Relationships & marriage
What pattern keeps repeating with different people?
Health
What has no clear medical explanation, or keeps recurring after treatment?
Family & household
What has your family always just accepted as "how things are"?
Spiritual life
What makes it hard to pray, focus, or feel close to God?
Sleep & dreams
What dream or sleep pattern keeps returning?
4. Compare what you're experiencing to what's expected

Deuteronomy 28 describes what a life walking in blessing is expected to look like in broad strokes. Laying that description next to your actual, ongoing experience is often the fastest way to see a pattern clearly.

Scripture's expected patternA persistent pattern worth naming
Fruitfulness — "blessed shall be the fruit of thy body" (Deut. 28:4, KJV)Barrenness, miscarriage, or repeated marriage delay
Provision and increase — "blessed shall be thy basket and thy store" (Deut. 28:5, KJV)A poverty pattern, or money that consistently "finishes" without explanation
Safety in ordinary life — "blessed when thou goest out, and blessed when thou comest in" (Deut. 28:6, KJV)Recurring accidents, near-misses, or a sense of attack while asleep
Success over opposition — "the LORD shall cause thine enemies... to be smitten before thy face" (Deut. 28:7, KJV)Closed doors, or success that keeps dissolving right before it arrives
Being the head, not the tail — "above only, and not beneath" (Deut. 28:13, KJV)Chronic stagnation, or a pattern of settling for less than what was worked for

This comparison is a lens for reflection, not a diagnosis on its own — hardship has many causes, and not every setback in this list means something spiritual is at work. It's meant to help you notice, not to alarm you.
